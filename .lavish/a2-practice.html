Practice — the wizard, and the summary step that is
the actually valuable half

Everything below is rendered in the app's own stylesheet, and every number, Arabic word and question in it came out of the real engine — I ran the plans through wordPool → relevance → questionStream and pasted the output. So the counts move the way they will actually move. Nothing is implemented yet; this is the thing to react to first. Decisions are recorded at the bottom, and every mock is annotatable.

Revised after your first pass. Five of the six decisions are answered and folded in — the wizard is drawn as five multi-field pages (Q1 A), the sample is a sample (Q2), the footer names the questions that changed (Q3), A2 does not touch quiz/ at all (Q4), and the wizard always opens at step 1 (Q5). Second pass: classic is untouched entirely — a verbatim file move (Q6), the Ready page's two cards are merged into one with an Edit button, and a tap that moves nothing is accepted as-is with a named comment. Where an answer went against my recommendation I have kept the cost visible rather than quietly rewriting it as the better idea.

00 Where A2 starts from

Today's Practice is one screen: 33 controls in 7 rows, and the panel that explains what they add up to sits underneath all of them. On a 375 pt phone the fold lands mid-way through the Abwāb row, so the feedback for a chip you just tapped is roughly a screen and a half below your thumb.

  1. 1Four quiz types, and not one of them says what a question looks like. "Identify" does not say identify what; "Meaning → verb" is an arrow you have to decode.
  2. 2The explanation is below everything that determines it. Tap a tense chip at the top and the sentence that tells you what just happened is off-screen.
The interesting number, and it is not 2,268 Dropping māḍī from that default — one tap — takes the count from 2,268 to 378. Not because there are fewer words (there are 756 → 378) but because losing a second tense retires the Tense question and the Bāb question at the same time. A 6× cliff from one chip, currently reported as a number that silently changed while off-screen. Making that legible is most of what this step is for.

02 The shared summary — what it renders, and what it deliberately doesn't

This is the half that survives whichever flow wins, so it gets designed first. The mock below is real output for identify · muḍāriʿ · maʿrūf · all three iʿrāb · Form I · sound + doubled: 1,134 cells, two live question kinds, 2,268 possible questions, and the sample is literally the first thing questionStream handed me for that plan.

Decided — classic is untouched entirely Not just its quiz-type row: no summary card at the top, no reordering, no relabelling. practice-classic.js is a verbatim move. That makes the flag a much starker comparison — the summary card, the sample question and the renamed types are all things only the wizard has, so what gets judged is the whole wizard proposition rather than a layout difference.

The one consequence worth a word from you. The same quiz type now has two names depending on the flag: classic says Identify and Meaning → verb, the wizard says Name the grammar and Match the meaning. The roadmap listed the rename as an A2 item, so this is me deliberately not doing half of it in order to honour "untouched entirely". The new names live in glossary.js · QUIZ_TYPE_INFO, so pointing classic at them later is a three-line diff — one import, one deletion of its local QUIZ_TYPES const, one map. Say the word.
  1. 1The sample question is the feature. It is drawn from the real questionStream for the real plan, rendered inert. Nothing written in a subtitle explains "Match the meaning" as well as one look at it.
  2. 2One card, two zones — what you chose, and what that makes it ask. You were right that the two were restating each other: "only one tense selected" is the Tense row saying its own value again. They share a border and a header now, and the values sit directly above the consequences. Chips are still grouped by axis, because a bare مَرْفُوع does not say which row it came from.
Decided from your annotation — one card, and one Edit button The two cards merged, "tap to change" became Edit, and Edit goes to step 1. That simplifies the component's interface more than it looks: it no longer needs a per-axis onJump(field) callback, just a single onEdit(). The value chips become display, not controls. It is also the coherent partner to Q5 — if the wizard always opens at step 1, "Edit" opening anywhere else would be the odd one out.

One consequence to accept: the retired reasons stay verbatim from QUESTION_RULES — "only one tense selected" still restates the Tense row a little — because Q4 keeps A2 out of quiz/, and those strings live there. Putting the value directly above the reason is as far as the view can go on its own.

The count and Start are not in the component

The roadmap lists the count as part of the summary step. I would keep it out of the summary file: the wizard already shows a running count in its footer on every step, and classic already shows one directly above Start, which is where it earns its keep ("an over-narrow selection is visible before you tap"). Putting it in the component means classic renders the same number twice, 800 px apart. So the component's job stays sayable in one sentence — "what this configuration will feel like" — and the host owns "how big it is and how to start it."

Regenerating the sample: the one real behavioural trap

Classic re-renders the whole screen on every chip tap. A freshly drawn question each time is visual noise, and worse, it re-rolls when you changed something that did not affect it. The pool walk is already paid for on every render (the count needs it), so this is about stability, not cost.

// ui/state.js — UI state, where the rest of the UI state lives
state.practice = {
  step: 'type',          // a STAGE ID, never an index — see §03
  sample: null,          // { planKey, question } — memo of exactly one
};

// practice-summary.js
function sampleFor(plan) {
  const key = JSON.stringify(plan);            // frozen + flat, so this is honest
  if (state.practice.sample?.planKey === key) return state.practice.sample.question;
  for (const q of questionStream(wordPool(plan))) {   // the REAL stream
    state.practice.sample = { planKey: key, question: q };
    return q;
  }
  return null;                                // dry pool — an absence, and the caller renders the gap
}

Change any axis → a new sample, which is informative ("oh, now I get iʿrāb questions"). Change nothing → the same card, so it does not flicker while you scroll. ↻ clears the memo.

Decided (Q2) — it is a sample, not question one The roadmap's "take question one from the actual stream" is satisfied by building it with the real questionStream over the real plan, then throwing it away. The run that starts afterwards draws its own first question. This is what keeps the summary from holding a half-consumed generator across renders, and it is what lets ↻ mean something.

03 The wizard — five pages, and the number in the footer

Decided (Q1) — five multi-field pages type → verbs + forms → tense + voice + iʿrāb → length → ready. Five Next taps, matching the roadmap's step list literally. What that costs, stated plainly rather than argued with: "skipped entirely" now operates at two different grains. A whole page disappears only for derived nouns; voice and iʿrāb become absent rows on the charts page, not absent steps. That still clears the exit criterion — nothing greys out — but a first-timer never sees the wizard visibly shorten when they pick the amr. Your Q3 answer is what pays for this, and §03 below shows why it stopped being optional.

The step table

Five entries. The cursor is a stage id, never an index: pick Derived nouns and the charts page vanishes, so an integer index into a list whose length just changed would land "Back" somewhere arbitrary.

// practice-wizard.js — the same shape as QUESTION_RULES, SETTINGS_SPEC, CHART_SHAPES.
// Adding a page is one object; `applies` is the ONLY place a page can disappear.
export const WIZARD_STEPS = [
  { id: 'type',   title: 'What do you want to practise?', applies: () => true, render: stepType },
  { id: 'verbs',  title: 'Which verbs?',                applies: () => true, render: stepVerbs },
  // A derived noun has no chart, so this whole page is absent — the same condition
  // practice.js already uses to hide those four rows today.
  { id: 'charts', title: 'Which charts?',               applies: (d) => d.quizType !== 'derived', render: stepCharts },
  { id: 'count',  title: 'How many questions?',         applies: () => true, render: stepCount },
  { id: 'ready',  title: 'Ready',                       applies: () => true, render: stepReady },
];

const visibleSteps = (d) => WIZARD_STEPS.filter((s) => s.applies(d));

// Row-level applicability INSIDE the charts page. Two conditions, both lifted
// verbatim from today's practice.js — the amr is neither passive nor iʿrāb-bearing,
// and only the muḍāriʿ has moods. Here they omit a row instead of greying one.
const hasVoice  = (d) => d.tenses.some((t) => t !== 'amr');
const hasMoods  = (d) => d.tenses.includes('mudari');
Skipped ≠ cleared A page or row that does not apply leaves state.draft alone. Choose Derived nouns, then go back to Name the grammar, and your tense/voice/iʿrāb selection is exactly as you left it. That matches the existing comment in quiz-plan.js about not nulling moods outside the muḍāriʿ — same rule, same reason: a skipped axis is not applicable, not unset.

The walk, with the counts it actually produces

Every footer number below came from running that exact draft through possibleQuestions(wordPool(quizPlan(d))). The delta line compares against the previous render, so on a multi-row page each tap still names what it did.

Decided (Q3) — and on a five-page wizard it stops being a nicety On a one-lever-per-page wizard the count alone almost tells the story. On a three-row charts page it does not: the two mocks above are the same page at 2,268 both times, with three question kinds having changed underneath. The line costs one field of UI state — the previous render's live-kind ids — and about eight lines. It is the thing that keeps §5.2b teachable now that the rows share a page.

Where the wizard opens on your fifth session

Decided (Q5) — always step 1 No summary-first shortcut. The consequence is the one the flag exists to measure: changing one chip on session five means walking all five pages, and if that is what sinks the wizard, it sinks it honestly rather than being papered over by a shortcut classic does not have. Concretely: state.practice.step is initialised to 'type' whenever Practice is entered, not persisted across visits. The summary chips still jump — from the Ready page you can tap a chip to go back to its step, which is a different thing from opening there.

04 The setting — and the gap it exposes in More

The roadmap wants practiceFlow at audience: 'user' precisely so it can be flipped at runtime. There is a catch worth naming: no user-audience setting in More is currently changeable. more.js renders them as read-only rows —

// screens/more.js, today — a value, and no handler
for (const s of userSettings()) {
  app.append(rowNav(s.label, String(settings[s.id])));   // ← no .onclick anywhere
}

So A2 has to build the first working user preference. The cheapest honest version keeps the registry declarative: an entry that carries options renders a segmented control; one that doesn't stays exactly as it is today. Additive, and the other three user rows are untouched until someone gives them options.

// settings/settings.js — one new entry, one new optional field
{ id: 'practiceFlow', audience: 'user', default: 'classic',
  label: 'Practice layout',
  options: [
    { value: 'classic', label: 'One screen' },
    { value: 'wizard',  label: 'Step by step' },
  ],
  note: 'both flows write the same QuizPlan; see ROADMAP A2' },

// screens/more.js — a row with options gets a control; one without stays read-only.
for (const s of userSettings()) {
  app.append(s.options
    ? settingRow(s, (v) => { setSetting(s.id, v); resetWizard(); rerender(); })
    : rowNav(s.label, String(settings[s.id])));
}

// ui/dom.js — new sibling of chipRow(). A chip row is a multi-select filter;
// this is a one-of-N switch, and they should not look alike.
export function segmented(options, isOn, onPick) { … }
The bug this prevents Flipping the flag while state.practice.step === 'charts' and then opening Practice drops you into step 3 of a wizard you just turned on. resetWizard() — one line — is not optional. Under Q5 the wizard resets on every entry anyway, so this is the same line doing double duty.

06 What A2 must not touch, and how we'd know

InvariantHow this design holds it
Both flows write the same QuizPlan, with no wizard-only fields Structural: neither flow calls quizPlan(). Both mutate state.draft; practice.js owns the single draftPlan() call on the start path. A smoke assertion can still pin it, but it cannot fail differently from the code.
Quiz generation, relevance and history are untouched Nothing below screens/ changes at all — that is now absolute, since you declined the revive strings (Q4). The summary reads questionStream, wordPool and relevance; it does not extend any of them. A2 is a screens-and-settings change and nothing else, which makes the parity snapshot a formality rather than a real risk.
Absence is a value A dry pool gives sampleFor() → null and the summary renders the gap ("No questions possible — widen the selection"). No placeholder question is ever invented.
Granular verb types in data, display groups in the view The wizard's verb step reuses the existing verbTypesInGroup() expansion verbatim. It is the same boundary, moved to its own screen.
Files stay under ~400 lines Largest is practice-wizard.js at roughly 190, most of it the eight step renderers.
The engine snapshot A2 touches no engine code, so the 20,252-line parity dump should be unnecessary — but it is cheap, and practice.js imports the pool walk. Run it anyway; the cost is one command.

Build order I'd follow

  • 1 · Split the files, change nothing else. Move startPlan() up, extract classic verbatim, add the flag. Green suite, identical screen. A commit that is provably a no-op.
  • 2 · glossary.js · QUIZ_TYPE_INFO and practice-summary.js. The strings get a shared home and the summary card gets built, even though only the wizard renders it.
  • 3 · The wizard, step table first, renderers second, summary card as step 5.
  • 4 · The More control and the reset-on-flip.

07 Risks I can see

A tap that changes nothing — accepted as-is

The voice row frequently moves neither the count nor the kinds: maʿrūf is already the only voice selected, so tapping it goes 378 → 378 with no delta line. You accepted that it looks broken for now rather than building an "unchanged" case. Recorded as a named comment where the footer is rendered, not only here:

// ACCEPTED (A2, Aug 2026): a chip that changes neither the count nor the
// live kinds renders no delta line and an identical number — tapping maʿrūf
// when it is already the only voice goes 378 → 378. It reads as a dead
// control. Deliberately NOT special-cased: an "unchanged, and here is why"
// branch would need to know WHY nothing moved, which is relevance()'s
// business, and Q4 keeps A2 out of quiz/.
Two flows means two places to forget something your question

To be precise about what I meant: لَنْ and لَمْ themselves are not classic-only. The registry is MUDARI_PARTICLES in meaning-service.js, and builders/from-meaning.js:67 reads it, so the particles reach the quiz cards and the feedback line whichever screen started the session.

What is classic-only is one sentence of pre-quiz copy — screens/practice.js:126–134, the note that says governed states are read through their particle, so “he did not help” and “he will not help” are separate answers. It is the only screen-level use of particleFor. Classic keeps its copy untouched; the wizard needs its own, in the summary card, or someone picking manṣūb there is never told why the meanings will look like that. So the note is duplicated across the two flows — deliberately, since classic is frozen and the two are being compared, not merged. If the wizard wins, classic is deleted and the duplicate goes with it.

The sample can be slower than it looks

wordPool() is already walked on every render, so the marginal cost is one draw(). But draw() retries up to 80 times and returns null on a nearly-dry pool — a configuration with 3 valid cells will sometimes show no card at all while claiming "≈ 3 possible questions". Needs a deliberate empty state, not a blank box.

Endless + summary

With count: 'endless' the Start button reads "Start · ∞", and the count line means something different (a pool size, not a quiz length). Minor, but the summary chip should say endless, not a number, and Start should not imply a finish line.
